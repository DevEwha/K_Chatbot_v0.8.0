\subsection{Cache Correctness and Repair Cost}
\label{sec:eval-kv}

We finally evaluate whether ProgressiveServe's boundary-aware KV repair is both necessary for correctness and cheap enough to preserve user-visible responsiveness.
This subsection focuses narrowly on the new KV-recomputation mechanism rather than on overall startup behavior.
Our goal is to isolate the consequence of \emph{not} repairing stale post-promotion KV, and then measure how much latency ProgressiveServe recovers by rematerializing only the affected suffix.
Figure~\ref{fig:kv-main} now shows as much of this story as the completed data supports: the left plot summarizes KV-correctness behavior across all four models and both promotions, while the middle and right plots use the completed LLaMA-2-7B origin-first runs to show the representative latency and repair-cost scaling for both Stage~\(1\!\rightarrow\!2\) and Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\).
Table~\ref{tab:kv-summary} complements the figure with the exact cross-model ratios.

\paragraph{Repair is necessary for correctness.}
We first ask whether KV states produced before promotion can be reused unchanged after later layers become active.
We use the lossless three-turn probe described in Section~\ref{sec:kv_reconcile}: the model processes span \(A\) at Stage~1, then we evaluate perplexity only on newly appended span \(B\) after the Stage~\(1\!\rightarrow\!2\) promotion and only on newly appended span \(C\) after the Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\) promotion.
We compare three policies:
\textit{Oracle-Recompute}, which fully recomputes the prefix under the promoted model;
\textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair}, which preserves the unchanged prefix and recomputes only the suffix beyond the promotion point;
and \textit{Stale-Reuse}, which reuses the pre-promotion KV state without repair.

The left plot in Figure~\ref{fig:kv-main} shows that stale reuse is consistently unsafe across all four completed models.
For the representative LLaMA-2-7B run, \textit{Stale-Reuse} increases perplexity by \(+9.31\%\) on \(B\) and \(+33.66\%\) on \(C\) relative to the oracle.
In contrast, \textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair} stays within \(-0.90\%\) on \(B\) and \(+0.18\%\) on \(C\), effectively matching the oracle despite reusing the unchanged lower-prefix state.
The same pattern generalizes across the full model set.
LLaMA-2-13B remains within \(-0.81\%\) and \(+0.99\%\) of oracle after repair, while stale reuse degrades by \(+7.23\%\) and \(+14.19\%\).
Falcon-7B remains within \(-1.35\%\) and \(+2.69\%\) after repair, while stale reuse degrades by \(+2.91\%\) and \(+30.24\%\).
Gemma-7B remains within \(+0.37\%\) and \(+0.72\%\) of oracle after repair, while stale reuse degrades by \(+12.86\%\) and \(+30.76\%\).
These gaps are too large to treat stale KV as a harmless approximation: once the model changes at promotion time, upper-layer KV must be repaired to recover semantically correct post-promotion state.

\paragraph{Repair cost is bounded and substantially lower than full reset.}
Correctness alone is not enough if repair simply pushes a full-prefix recomputation penalty onto the next user interaction.
We therefore sweep accumulated prefix length \(T\) in the completed origin-first LLaMA-2-7B runs and compare \textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair} against a correctness-preserving \textit{Full-Reset} baseline that discards the prefix cache and lets the next request re-prefill under the promoted model.

The middle plot in Figure~\ref{fig:kv-main} shows that \textit{Full-Reset} grows rapidly with prefix length for both promotions, while \textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair} stays nearly flat.
For the representative Stage~\(1\!\rightarrow\!2\) transition, the first post-promotion request TTFT grows from \(65.1\)~ms to \(227.7\)~ms under \textit{Full-Reset} as \(T\) increases from \(200\) to \(1500\), whereas \textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair} stays between \(48.4\)~ms and \(56.7\)~ms over the same range.
At the largest tested context, ProgressiveServe reduces the first post-promotion request TTFT from \(227.7\)~ms to \(56.7\)~ms, a \(4.02\times\) improvement.
The right plot in Figure~\ref{fig:kv-main} shows the corresponding normalized repair overhead for both promotions.
Even at \(T{=}1500\), the repair path remains bounded at \(95.75\)~\(\mu\)s/token with a \(100.0\%\) fast-path success rate.

The later Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\) transition shows the same qualitative behavior.
At \(T{=}1500\), ProgressiveServe reduces the first post-promotion request TTFT from \(228.5\)~ms to \(54.4\)~ms, a \(4.20\times\) improvement, while the normalized repair overhead is \(71.8\)~\(\mu\)s/token.
The broader completed origin-first sweep shows the same trend on larger and different model families.
At \(T_{\max}\), LLaMA-2-13B reduces the first post-promotion request TTFT from \(410.8\)~ms to \(89.1\)~ms at Stage~\(1\!\rightarrow\!2\) and from \(415.6\)~ms to \(89.0\)~ms at Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\), corresponding to \(4.61\times\) and \(4.67\times\) improvements with normalized repair overheads of \(137.45\) and \(109.04\)~\(\mu\)s/token, respectively.
Gemma-7B similarly reduces TTFT from \(270.8\)~ms to \(66.2\)~ms at Stage~\(1\!\rightarrow\!2\) and from \(273.1\)~ms to \(65.3\)~ms at Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\), corresponding to \(4.09\times\) and \(4.18\times\) improvements with normalized repair overheads of \(214.02\) and \(199.02\)~\(\mu\)s/token.
All completed origin-first timing runs maintain a \(100.0\%\) fast-path success rate.
Thus, the repair mechanism does not merely preserve correctness; it also prevents promotion from manifesting as a long full-prefix stall on the next turn.

\paragraph{Cross-model summary.}
Table~\ref{tab:kv-summary} summarizes the completed four-model correctness sweep together with the currently completed post-promotion gain measurements.
Across all four models, \textit{ProgressiveServe-Repair} remains close to the oracle while \textit{Stale-Reuse} consistently degrades perplexity, especially after the later Stage~\(2\!\rightarrow\!3\) promotion.
The origin-first request-side gain sweep is now complete for LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, and Gemma-7B, so Table~\ref{tab:kv-summary} fills those gain entries directly and leaves only Falcon-7B as \textit{?}.
Normalized repair work is still reported in the text and in Figure~\ref{fig:kv-main}(right), but we omit it from the main table because it is an internal per-token work metric rather than a direct user-visible latency metric.
This strengthens the main systems claim of this subsection: correctness requires targeted KV repair, and across the completed timing runs the request-side benefit remains around \(4\times\) at long prefixes.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Boundary-aware KV repair is not an optional optimization layered on top of progressive serving.
Without repair, stale upper-layer KV measurably corrupts the post-promotion model state across all four models.
With repair, ProgressiveServe recovers oracle-level correctness to within about \(1\%\) on both LLaMA variants and Gemma, and within \(2.69\%\) even on Falcon, while cutting the first post-promotion request latency by about \(4\times\) at long prefixes across all currently completed origin-first timing runs.
